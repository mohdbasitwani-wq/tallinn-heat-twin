How the forecast works

Heating load is, within one operating mode, close to linear in outdoor temperature — so the engine fits today's 24 observed pairs to a degree-hour line, load ≈ a + b·max(0, 17−T_out), by least squares. The hour-by-hour leftover (the diurnal / hot-water / setback signature) is kept as a 24-hour-lag shape and added back. Tomorrow's prediction re-evaluates the temperature term on the Tallinn forecast and re-applies that shape. Literature finds temperature at 1 h and 24 h lags are the dominant predictors and that this clustered-regression approach matches neural nets for heating, which is why a transparent model is used here instead of a black box.
